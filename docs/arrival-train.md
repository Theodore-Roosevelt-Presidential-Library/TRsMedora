# The train Roosevelt arrived on

Research note, October 15, 2026. Train name and equipment are **not yet confirmed**.

## What is known

- T.R. reached Little Missouri station "about three in the morning" in September 1883 (AUTO ch. IV). The TR Center gives the night of September 7–8. A Library OTD post said "shortly after two." **Reconcile before publishing.**
- He came west from Chicago and St. Paul on the Northern Pacific's regular through westbound passenger train. A forum post citing the 1883 *Official Guide* says the through train west left Minneapolis at 8:45 p.m. That fits a 3 a.m. arrival two nights later. The Guide itself has not been checked.
- **Train name is unconfirmed.**
  - The NP's own Fall 1883 Rand McNally map shows the westbound train as the "Northern Pacific Express."
  - From 1884 on, timetables call it the "Pacific Express."
  - Hagedorn's "the Overland" is a loose description, not a train name.
- **The dining car is uncertain.** Hagedorn's scene of a gunman demanding "cow-grease" in the dining car describes the Villard excursion train the night before, not T.R.'s train. The NP's through dining-car service in the West began September 12, 1883 (Railway Age), after T.R. arrived.
- **Locomotive and cars:** NP passenger trains of 1883 were pulled by 4-4-0 "American" engines from several builders, including Baldwin, Portland, Taunton, Hinkley and Manchester. They hauled coaches and Pullman sleepers.
