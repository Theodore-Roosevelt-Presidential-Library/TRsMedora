# Questions for Rolf Sletten: Medora 1883–87 in 3D

*Prepared Oct 17, 2026, for Matt Briney (Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library). Kept in the repo as the single list of questions the photographs and papers can't settle; update it as answers come in.*

## About the project

The Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library is rebuilding Medora, Little Missouri and the Chateau de Mores as they stood in 1883–87, as a 2D map and a 3D model: [trsmedora.labs.trlibrary.com](https://trsmedora.labs.trlibrary.com). Every building is placed from period photographs, the 1883 plat, the *Bad Lands Cow Boy* and later histories, and each one carries a grade for how sure the placement is.

The questions below are the ones the photographs and papers can't settle. Any answer helps, even a partial one: a lot or block number, a side of the street, a date, a name, or a source to look in. Where a question gives a position, "local metres" means metres east and north of a fixed point near the depot; a lot, block or landmark is just as useful in reply.

## 2. Packing plant and stockyards

- **2.1 Plant layout.** The photos put the main packing house about 14 degrees off due east (long axis running east-north-east), about 33 m long and 10 m to the eave, with a lower annex at its west end, an enclosed runway to a timber pier, the cooling house behind its east end, an ice house to the west and a dark building with a tower to the east. Does that match what's known of the foundations or the 1950s NPS plans? Where can we get those plans?
- **2.1a Galleries.** The 1885 photo shows open galleries along the east end of the plant; by 1886 they appear gone. Were they construction staging?
- **2.2 Plant buildings.** What were the "east building" with two roof ventilators and the tall dark tower beside it? A panorama suggests both sat about 5 m further west, right against the main block.
- **2.3 Chimney height.** The photos give about 23 m (estimates range from 21 to 27 m). Is the original height recorded?
- **2.4 Spur track.** Where did the plant's spur and the cooling house's loading siding run?
- **2.5 Stockyards.** A c.1890s view from the south-east hills shows a two-pen stockyard with two tall loading chutes, about 115 m south of the main line. The Cow Boy says the old NP yards "across the river" were too small, a 50 x 50 then 100 x 100 ft yard was built (April 1885), and new yards went up at "the brick-kiln butte," using the river, butte and railroad as pen walls (October 1885). Is the yard in the photo the 1885 one? Where was the brick-kiln butte? Did a stock siding run beside it?
- **2.6 Little Missouri shipping corrals.** "The shipping corrals near the Cantonment" (Hagedorn): where were they?

## 3. Surviving buildings

- **3.1 St. Mary's Church.** In the c.1886 photo the roof reads slightly steeper and the belfry about 0.5 m higher than today. In a c.1890s view the church reads about 8% shorter than today's footprint. Has the church been lengthened, re-roofed or had its belfry rebuilt? The side windows were pointed in 1886 and are round-headed now: when did that change?
- **3.2 Von Hoffman House.** What was the original wall colour, and what was the north end of the roof like? (SHSND 00098-00019, catalogued as "Foley's house", is the Von Hoffman House c.1910s-30s: mid-tone brick, a clipped-gable roof with a porch across the gable end.)
- **3.3 Chateau stable and outbuildings.** The NRHP puts the coachman's house, carriage shed, stable and corral "to the south and further east, on the next terrace towards the river." The 1883 photo shows two gabled sheds in the bottom land about 100–120 m south of the house, and nothing at the spot we had. Where did each outbuilding stand? (Update Oct 17: the 1884 photo puts a long low stable or carriage shed on the upper terrace about 45 m west-north-west of the house; is that right?)
- **3.3a Chateau service wing.** The hunting-party photo (00042-00191) and 00098-00018 show the north side in the 1880s: a 1½-storey wing with its ridge parallel to the main block and an east-facing gable, lean-tos, and no windows at the east end of the main north wall. Today's wing (and our model) has a north-facing gable. When was the wing rebuilt?
- **3.4 Metropolitan Hotel / Rough Riders.** The photos show a dark two-storey building with its long side east-west, about 22 x 10 m, a low hipped roof and paired windows; it is white by about 1905. Which side was the front and porch on? Was it painted between 1886 and 1901?

## 4. Little Missouri and the railroad

- **4.1 Pyramid Park Hotel.** From the Haynes 1881 photo it stood about 38 m north of the main track and about 50 m east of the depot's west end, with a 14 m frontage. It is gone in a view from the 1890s. When was it torn down or moved?
- **4.2 Tracks at Little Missouri.** Haynes 1881 shows three tracks. Which one was the main line?
- **4.3 Track east of the river.** Was the NP line through Medora moved after 1883?
- **4.4 Medora depot.** By the 1890s the depot reads taller. Was it rebuilt or given a second storey? Can anyone read the mileboards (about "595.3 M" to St. Paul)?
- **4.5 Hotels and houses.** Where were Peter Malloy's hotel at Little Missouri (the Western House?), the Pioneer Hotel ("close to the depot"), the NP section house and water tank, the Tiser House, Gifford Bros. store, the 1883 saloons and Paddock's 1880 cabin?
- **4.6 The Cantonment.** Haynes's 1880 photo shows a log building about 97 x 22 ft, long axis WNW-ESE, about 90 m west of where the 1883 plat's symbols put the post. Which building is it, and how were the buildings laid out around the parade ground?
- **4.7 The bridge.** Haynes's 1880 photo and Huffman's 1886 view show a long pile trestle (we model about 210 m on a raised grade). How long was the original NP bridge at Little Missouri, and when was it replaced by fill?
- **4.8 The river.** A late-1880s panorama shows a water channel running between the track and the packing plant. Was that the river's main channel then, or a slough? We now show a narrow slough north of the track and the main channel looping south of it in 1880-86.
